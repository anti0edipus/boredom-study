EXPERIMENT FLOW — BOREDOM & SES STUDY
======================================


OVERVIEW
--------

A single-session, three-arm between-subjects experiment examining how different types
of writing manipulations influence subsequent boredom experience. Participants are
recruited via Prolific and randomly assigned to one of three conditions: Meaning,
Autonomy, or Control.

Estimated duration: 35-45 minutes


STEP-BY-STEP FLOW
-----------------


0. ARRIVAL

The participant clicks the Prolific study link. Their Prolific ID, Study ID, and
Session ID are captured silently from the URL. They are immediately redirected to the
consent page. No substantive data is recorded at this point beyond arrival time and
browser information.


1. CONSENT

The participant is shown the full IRB consent text. They must tick an explicit checkbox
confirming they consent before the "Begin" button activates. Closing the window at this
stage saves no data beyond a refusal flag.


PAGE 1. DEMOGRAPHICS & SES

  Variable                 Format
  -----------------------  -------------------------------------------------------
  Gender                   Categorical (Male / Female / Non-binary / Prefer not)
  Age                      Numeric (years)
  Relationship status      Categorical (10 options + prefer not)
  Ethnicity                Hispanic/Latino — Yes / No / Prefer not
  Race                     Multi-select checkboxes (7 options incl. Other + text)
  Household income         Ordinal (13 USD bands + prefer not)
  Personal income          Ordinal (13 USD bands + prefer not)
  Education                Ordinal (11 US levels + prefer not)
  Employment               Multi-select checkboxes (6 options)
  Job title                Free text (optional)

  Subjective SES Ladders — Adler et al. (2000); 10=most/best, 1=least/worst
  -------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ladder_education         How much education (visual ladder, 1–10)
  ladder_money             How much money (visual ladder, 1–10)
  ladder_job               How good a job (visual ladder, 1–10)

  Sociometric Status Ladders — Mahadevan et al. (2021); 10=most, 1=least
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  sociometric_respect      How respected (visual ladder, 1–10)
  sociometric_admired      How admired (visual ladder, 1–10)
  sociometric_important    How important (visual ladder, 1–10)

CONDITION ASSIGNMENT (invisible to participant)

Between the demographics page and the trait scales, the server silently assigns the
participant to one of three conditions using permuted-block randomization (block sizes
randomly drawn from {3, 6, 9}). The assignment is recorded in the database before the
participant sees any condition-specific content. The participant is never informed of
their condition. Refreshing the browser does not change the assignment.

  Condition   Description
  ----------  ----------------------------------------------------------------
  Meaning     Write about intrinsic, self-defining life goals and what makes
              life meaningful
  Autonomy    Recall and write about a recent freely chosen, self-endorsed
              action
  Control     Describe a typical morning routine in detail (neutral task)


PAGE 2. TRAIT SCALES

Six questionnaire blocks presented in randomized block order (each participant sees
them in a different sequence). Within each scale, item order is also randomized.
The display order is recorded in the data for analysis.

  Scale                                         Items   Response format
  --------------------------------------------  ------  -----------------------------------------------
  Short Boredom Proneness Scale (SBPS)          8       7-point (Strongly disagree – Strongly agree)
    Struk et al. (2017)
  Presence of Meaning in Life (MLQ-P)           5       7-point (Absolutely untrue – Absolutely true)
    Steger et al. (2006)
  Subjective Personal Agency (SPA)              5       5-point (Strongly disagree – Strongly agree)
    Yamaguchi et al. (2025)
  BPNS Autonomy Subscale                        7       7-point (Not at all true – Very true)
    Deci & Ryan (2000); Johnston & Finney (2010)
  BPNSF Autonomy Satisfaction & Frustration     8       5-point (Not true at all – Completely true)
    Chen et al. (2015)
  General Self-Efficacy Scale (GSE)             10      4-point (Not true at all – Exactly true)
    Schwarzer & Jerusalem (1995)

One attention check item is embedded in the SBPS block ("Please select '4 – Somewhat
agree' for this item"). Participants who fail this item are flagged in the data
(attention_check_pass = 0) but are not excluded automatically — exclusion decisions
are left to the researcher at the analysis stage.


PAGE 3. WRITING TASK

A large text box is shown with a condition-specific prompt (see above). Two criteria
must both be met before the participant can continue:

  - Minimum time on page: 3 minutes (180 seconds). A countdown timer is visible;
    the submit button is disabled until it reaches zero.
  - Minimum character count: 400 characters. The button also remains disabled until
    this threshold is reached.

Data recorded: full essay text, time spent on page (seconds), character count.


PAGE 4. MANIPULATION CHECK

8 items measuring current psychological state, presented in randomized order immediately
after the writing task and before the boring task:

  Subscale          Items   Response format
  ----------------  ------  --------------------------------
  State Meaning     4       7-point Likert
  State Autonomy    4       7-point Likert

This check allows verification that the writing manipulation successfully induced the
intended psychological state and supports analysis of the meaning x autonomy double
dissociation.


PAGE 5. BORING TASK — BOREDOM INDUCTION

A 5-minute text transcription task:

  - Participants are shown an image of a passage of dense technical text
    (an ASTM standards table)
  - They must type out the text as accurately as possible in a text box
  - The image is displayed to the left; the text box to the right (side-by-side layout)
  - Copy-paste is prevented by using an image rather than selectable text
  - A countdown timer is visible; the submit button is locked for the full 5 minutes
  - Transcription accuracy is computed server-side using difflib.SequenceMatcher
    (a score from 0.0 to 1.0)

Data recorded:

  Variable                    Description
  --------------------------  ---------------------------------------------------
  boringtask_duration_sec     Actual time on task (seconds)
  transcription_text          Full text typed by participant
  transcription_accuracy      Similarity to target text (0.0–1.0)
  transcription_charcount     Character count of typed text


PAGE 6. FINAL SURVEY — PRIMARY OUTCOME MEASURE

The Multidimensional State Boredom Scale short form (MSBS-8; Hunter et al., 2015) —
8 items, 7-point Likert scale. Items are presented in randomized order.

Example items: "I feel bored.", "Time is passing by slower than usual.",
"I am wasting time that would be better spent on something else."

This is the primary dependent variable: the hypothesis is that the meaning and/or
autonomy writing conditions will reduce state boredom relative to the control condition
following the boring task.


PAGE 7. DEBRIEF & COMPLETION

The participant reads the full IRB-approved debrief text explaining the study's purpose.
They click "Submit & return to Prolific", which:

  1. Marks their record as completed in the database
  2. Records the finish timestamp and computes total study duration
  3. Redirects them to the Prolific completion URL, triggering their payment

DROPOUT HANDLING
----------------

Partial data is saved to the database after every page submission. Participants who
close their browser partway through appear in the data with completed = 0. Their
partial responses up to the last completed page are retained and can be used for
attrition analyses.


RANDOMIZATION
-------------

Participants are assigned server-side using permuted-block randomization with block
sizes randomly drawn from {3, 6, 9}. Within each block, all three conditions appear
in equal numbers in random order. This ensures the three arms remain closely balanced
throughout data collection — the maximum imbalance at any point is bounded by the
largest block size (9), meaning no arm can be more than 3 participants ahead of another.
Assignment is idempotent: a participant who refreshes their browser keeps the same
condition.
